\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced a solver-agnostic framework that decomposes polynomial systems
into smaller branches by enumerating minimal hitting sets of their factor sets.

We proved that refinement terminates and preserves the full solution set, and
that the variants return the minimal primes, a generating set of the ideal,
and a minimal primary decomposition.

On the baseline benchmarks, factor branching completed 19 of 20
systems, compared with
9 for direct Gr\"obner-basis methods and 16 for decomposition
methods, and on the size-graded families 21 of 25, with a lead that grew with
the system size. On the QFT system, where one coupling must be nonzero,
imposing this by
saturation lets factor branching complete with all six
backends in under one second, while all nine standalone baselines
time out. Primary factor branching decomposes the same saturated system in
under five minutes.

Future work will find additional ways to break down polynomial systems into smaller branches which apply to a wider range of systems.
