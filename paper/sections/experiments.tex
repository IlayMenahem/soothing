\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}

We implemented Algorithm~\ref{alg:Branching} in Rust 1.97.1 and used
Symbolica 1.5 for polynomial arithmetic over the rationals. A branch can be checked by
M4GB, F5, GVW, Symbolica's built-in Gr\"obner basis implementation, or
Singular 4.4.1.  The comparison also includes six direct whole-ideal
Gr\"obner basis configurations, three decomposition methods from Singular, Macaulay2 1.26.06, and a numerical method from HomotopyContinuation.jl under Julia 1.12.6.

We ran the benchmark on an Apple M4 Pro with 14 CPU cores and 24 GB of memory.
Every table entry is a single run. Every method received 60 seconds per system.

For branching, $T$ is end-to-end wall time and $F$ is the time from the start
of the factor-cover search to its first satisfiable cover.  Factorization and
input processing are therefore included in $T$ but not in $F$.
The complete measurements are stored in the appendix.

\subsection{Baseline systems}

The baseline has 20 generated systems in six families. Signed-distance cycles and
affine-factor hypergraphs test many inexpensive covers.  The overlapping
random multiples use seeded dense quadratic factors that recur across
equations, with no factor shared by every equation.  In the gated systems, a
single selector multiplies every equation of a Cyclic, Katsura, or dense
quadratic system.  This produces one easy selector cover and one nonlinear
residual cover.

The larger gated systems expose the cost of the nonlinear residual cover that
is hidden by the easy selector cover.  At Cyclic-7, every branching backend
finds the selector component immediately but fails to exhaust both covers in
60 seconds; all direct Gr\"obner methods also time out, while the numerical
method completes in $58.287$ seconds.  For Katsura-8, branching with Singular
still completes in $2.657$ seconds, compared with $3.596$ seconds for facstd,
and every direct method times out.  Dense $n=7$ is harder: branching with
Singular takes $42.020$ seconds and HomotopyContinuation.jl takes $32.261$
seconds, while all direct methods time out.  Despite this increase in total
work, branching finds the first satisfiable selector cover within $0.052$
seconds on every larger gated system.
