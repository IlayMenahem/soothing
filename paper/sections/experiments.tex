\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:experimental-setup}

We implemented Algorithm~\ref{alg:Branching} in Rust 1.98.1 with Symbolica
3.0.1~\cite{ruijl2023symbolica}. Refinement prunes as in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:branching-framework}, and the branch solver computes a
Gr\"obner basis of each terminal branch, fewest generators first, with M4GB,
F5, GVW, F4, or Singular 4.4.1~\cite{makarim2017m4gb,faugere2002f5,
gao2016gvw,faugere1999f4,singular2025}; a basis containing $1$ certifies
UNSAT. The baselines are six whole-ideal Gr\"obner-basis configurations and
three decomposition methods from Singular and Macaulay2
1.26.06~\cite{singular2025,macaulay2}. Every entry is one run on an Apple M4
Pro (14 cores, 24 GB) with a 60-second limit. For factor branching, $T$ is the
wall time and $F$ the time from the start of the hitting-set search to the
first SAT branch. The measurements are in the \texttt{results} directory of the
repository listed under Data availability.

\subsection{Baseline systems}
\label{subsec:baseline-systems}

The baseline has 20 generated systems in six families
(Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-families}): signed-distance cycles and affine-factor
hypergraphs with many cheap branches, overlapping random multiples whose dense
quadratic factors recur across equations, and gated systems, which multiply
every equation of Cyclic, Katsura, or a dense quadratic system by a new
variable~\cite{bjorck1991cyclic,boege1986examples} and so have one easy
selector branch and one nonlinear residual branch.

\subsection{Size-graded families}
\label{subsec:scaling-families}

% [Families whose factors come from the problem, grown until every
% method times out]
The following families inherit their factors from the problem, and we
grow each until every method times out.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The reduced cyclic $n$-roots systems \texttt{Singular.rcyclic\_$n$}, a
    variant of the cyclic $n$-roots problem~\cite{bjorck1991cyclic}, taken
    unchanged from
    SymbolicData\footnote{\url{https://github.com/symbolicdata/data},
    commit \texttt{a5cdf53}.}.
  \item Nash equilibria of $n$-player games with two pure strategies per
    player~\cite{sturmfels2002solving}.  Each player $i$ either plays a pure
    strategy, $p_i(p_i-1)=0$, or is indifferent, so its payoff difference
    vanishes.  The payoffs are random integers in $[-9,9]$ (generic) or in
    $\{-1,0,1\}$ (degenerate).
  \item Critical points of a random quadratic program $f$ on every face of the
    box $[0,1]^n$, given by $x_i(x_i-1)\,\partial_i f=0$.
  \item Equilibria of a random Lotka--Volterra community on every species
    support~\cite{hofbauer1998evolutionary}, given by
    $x_i\bigl(r_i+\sum_j a_{ij}x_j\bigr)=0$ with $a_{ii}<0$.
\end{itemize}
Each size is one seeded instance. Factor branching entries give $T$ only, the
direct class adds Singular's \texttt{std} in degrevlex (Singular dp) and drops
standalone F4, and a method that times out is not run at larger sizes.

\subsection{Physical system}
\label{subsec:physical-system}

The physical system is an order-five approximation that constrains graviton
couplings in asymptotic-safety models to remain consistent with quantum field
theory (QFT)~\cite{rafiezinedine2018simplifying,vacca2010asymptotic}; solving
it helps determine admissible fixed-point structures in quantum
gravity~\cite{vacca2010asymptotic}. It has 28 equations in 21 variables over
$\mathbb Q(\pi)$, and $\kappa_h$ must be nonzero. Factor branching and the
Singular and Macaulay2 baselines saturate by $\kappa_h$; the standalone M4GB,
F5, GVW, and F4 runs add the Rabinowitsch generator
$1-s\kappa_h$~\cite{rabinowitsch1930nullstellensatz}.

Every run on the physical systems has a 120-second limit, or 300 seconds in
Table~\ref{tab:physical-decomposition}, runs concurrently with the others, and
reports its internal solver time. Singular and Macaulay2
compute over $\mathbb Q(\pi)$, except Macaulay2's \texttt{decompose}, which
cannot, and the Rust programs, which take $\pi$ as a variable. Every
saturation uses Singular's \texttt{sat} or Macaulay2's \texttt{saturate}, every
primary decomposition \texttt{primdecSY}~\cite{shimoyama1996localization}, and
Algorithm~\ref{alg:PrimaryBranching} runs its separators. On the physical
system, saturations and decompositions use lex order with the min-fill
variable order, a greedy elimination order computed from the variable supports
of the equations, as refinement and Algorithm~\ref{alg:IdealReduce} always do.
Factor branching limits its saturation of the system to five seconds, removes
$\kappa_h$ from every factor set, drops a branch once $\kappa_h$ reduces to
zero modulo its generators, and adds $1-s\kappa_h$ to each terminal branch.

All nine standalone baselines time out on the physical system, whereas factor
branching, with every backend, refines the saturated system into 10 SAT
terminal branches and solves them in 0.87 to 0.94 seconds, about 0.83 seconds
of which go to the saturation by $\kappa_h$.

% Nonzero conditions: the rounds keep the saturation instead of the ideal
Keeping $\kappa_h$ nonzero changes only the SAT test of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:IdealReduce}: a minimal hitting set $H$ counts as SAT when
$V(H)\not\subseteq V(\kappa_h)$. The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sat-branches}
then shows that $Q$ and the cofactors of $h$ have no common zero outside
$V(\kappa_h)$, so the Nullstellensatz gives
$\kappa_h^sh^{a_h}\in\langle Q\rangle$ for some $s\geq0$. Each round then
keeps $\langle Q\rangle:\kappa_h^\infty$ instead of $\langle Q\rangle$, and
the termination argument of Theorem~\ref{thm:ideal-reduce} is unchanged.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:PrimaryBranching} then decomposes
$\langle Q\rangle:\kappa_h^\infty$, whose minimal primes are those of
$\langle Q\rangle$ that do not contain $\kappa_h$.

% [PrimaryBranching: the primary decomposition of the physical system]
Without a time limit, Algorithm~\ref{alg:PrimaryBranching} returns a minimal
primary decomposition of the physical system over $\mathbb Q(\pi)$.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:IdealReduce}, keeping $\kappa_h$ nonzero, reduces the 28 equations to 18
generators in three rounds and 0.6 seconds; Algorithm~\ref{alg:Branching} with
$\mathcal B_{\min}$ finds the six minimal primes in 0.1 seconds; the
separators give their isolated components in 1.6 seconds, but these do not
intersect to the ideal; and \texttt{primdecSY} decomposes the saturation, with
56 generators, in most of the time of Primary in
Table~\ref{tab:physical-decomposition}, whereas on the unreduced system with a
Rabinowitsch generator it did not finish in 30 minutes. Six of the ten components are isolated, in two families of
dimensions 13, 12, and 8: prime in one family, and in the other obtained from
the radical by squaring one generator, a variable, which doubles the degree. Each family also
has two embedded components, of dimension 10 inside its 12-dimensional
component and of dimension 8 inside its 13-dimensional one, and a Gr\"obner
basis of the saturation confirms that the components intersect to
$\langle P\rangle:\kappa_h^\infty$. With the variables in alphabetical order
instead, Algorithm~\ref{alg:IdealReduce} stops at 27 generators, because the factorizing Gr\"obner basis of the largest
hitting set does not finish within 120 seconds.

% [Table 4's pairs on the physical system and the graviton-only and
% fermion-only systems]
For each $\alpha\in\{1,1/2,1/4,2,4\}$, we also derive two systems from the
Einstein--Hilbert truncation of gravity coupled to fermions and scalars, each
with 31 equations once the Rabinowitsch equation of \texttt{KappaRefFinal} is
replaced by saturation. The graviton-only system sets $d_1=d_2=d_3=0$ (29 or
30 variables), and the fermion-only system sets every variable whose name
begins with $Dp$ or $p$ to zero (15 variables). On these systems, saturations and
decompositions use degrevlex with the variables in name order, which is faster
there. Table~\ref{tab:physical-decomposition} runs the pairs of
Table~\ref{tab:scaling-decomposition} on them and on the physical system.
