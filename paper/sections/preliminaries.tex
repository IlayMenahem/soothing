\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:preliminaries}

Let $K$ be a computable field and let
$R=K[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$.  The implementation considered in this paper uses
$K=\mathbb{Q}$ and exact arithmetic.  Geometric statements are made over an
algebraic closure $\overline K$.  For a finite set $F\subset R$, write
\[
  \langle F\rangle
    =\left\{\sum_{f\in F}a_f f : a_f\in R\right\},
  \qquad
  V(F)
    =\{a\in\overline K^n : f(a)=0\text{ for every }f\in F\}.
\]
Thus, throughout the paper, a polynomial system is \emph{satisfiable} if its
zero set in $\overline K^n$ is nonempty.  This convention is important: over
an algebraically closed field, Hilbert's Nullstellensatz gives
\begin{equation}
  V(F)=\varnothing
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  1\in\langle F\rangle .
  \label{eq:nullstellensatz-certificate}
\end{equation}
Consequently, consistency can be decided by an exact Gr\"obner-basis
computation.  The same equivalence does not hold for real feasibility without
additional real-algebraic machinery; real solution sets are not considered
here.  We use standard terminology from computational commutative algebra as
in~\cite{cox2015ideals,kreuzer2000computational}.

\subsection{Gr\"obner bases and interreduction}
\label{subsec:groebner-interreduction}

Fix an admissible monomial order $\prec$ on $R$.  For a nonzero polynomial
$f$, denote its leading monomial by $\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(f)$.  A finite
set $G\subset I$ is a Gr\"obner basis of an ideal $I$ if
\[
  \langle\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(g):g\in G\rangle
  =
  \langle\operatorname{LM}_{\prec}(f):f\in I\setminus\{0\}\rangle.
\]
Polynomial reduction by $G$ then supplies canonical normal forms when $G$ is
reduced.  In particular, the reduced Gr\"obner basis of the unit ideal is
$\{1\}$, so~\eqref{eq:nullstellensatz-certificate} is detected directly.
Lexicographic orders are useful for elimination, whereas degree-reverse-
lexicographic order is typically preferable for the consistency computations
in the inner search.  The correctness arguments below are independent of the
chosen order.

Before factorization, the input generators may be \emph{interreduced}: each
generator is replaced by its remainder modulo the others, zero remainders are
discarded, and this process is repeated to a fixed point.  A single replacement
$p_i\leftarrow p_i-q p_j$ preserves the generated ideal, since
$p_i=(p_i-q p_j)+q p_j$.  Hence sequential interreduction changes the
presentation, but neither the ideal nor its zero set.  This distinction matters
because the factors exposed by preprocessing, and therefore the subsequent
search space, may depend on the chosen generating set.

\subsection{Factor hypergraphs and minimal covers}
\label{subsec:factor-covers}

Let $P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_m\}\subset R$.  After removing zero generators, write a
complete factorization over $K$ as
\begin{equation}
  p_i=c_i\prod_{q\in C_i}q^{e_{i,q}},
  \qquad c_i\in K^*,\quad e_{i,q}\geq 1,
  \label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
where $C_i$ is the set of distinct, nonconstant irreducible factors of $p_i$.
Multiplicities play no role in a zero set and are therefore suppressed.  A
nonzero constant equation has $C_i=\varnothing$ and makes the system
inconsistent, whereas a zero equation imposes no constraint and is omitted.

The family $\mathcal{C}=\{C_1,\ldots,C_m\}$ is a hypergraph on the universe
$Q=\bigcup_i C_i$.  A set $T\subseteq Q$ is a \emph{factor cover}, or
equivalently a transversal (hitting set) of $\mathcal{C}$, if
$T\cap C_i\neq\varnothing$ for every $i$.  It is \emph{minimal} if none of its
proper subsets is a factor cover.  We denote the family of minimal factor
covers by $\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{C})$.  These are inclusion-minimal, not
necessarily minimum-cardinality, transversals~\cite{berge1989hypergraphs}.

The following elementary identity is the basis of the outer branching method.

\begin{lemma}[Factor-cover decomposition]
\label{lem:factor-cover-decomposition}
For the factorization in~\eqref{eq:factorization},
\begin{equation}
  V(P)
  =\bigcup_{T\in\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{C})}V(T).
  \label{eq:factor-cover-decomposition}
\end{equation}
Equivalently,
\[
  \sqrt{\langle P\rangle}
  =\bigcap_{T\in\operatorname{Tr}(\mathcal{C})}
     \sqrt{\langle T\rangle}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $a\in V(T)$ and $T$ hits every $C_i$, then some factor of every $p_i$
vanishes at $a$; hence $a\in V(P)$.  Conversely, if $a\in V(P)$, the set
$Z_a=\{q\in Q:q(a)=0\}$ hits every $C_i$.  Since the hypergraph is finite,
$Z_a$ contains an inclusion-minimal transversal $T$, and $a\in V(T)$.  The
ideal identity follows from the correspondence between finite unions of
algebraic sets and intersections of their radical ideals.
\end{proof}

A factor cover need not define an irreducible component: $\langle T\rangle$
may itself be reducible, and different covers may define nested or overlapping
subvarieties.  Accordingly, the method computes structured subvarieties whose
union is $V(P)$, rather than claiming an irredundant primary decomposition.
Restricting the enumeration to minimal covers loses no points, because if
$T'\supset T$, then $V(T')\subseteq V(T)$.

\subsection{Learned inconsistency and structure hints}
\label{subsec:cores-hints}

An \emph{unsatisfiable core} of a finite constraint set $F\subset R$ is a
subset $U\subseteq F$ for which $V(U)=\varnothing$, equivalently
$1\in\langle U\rangle$.  It is minimal if every proper subset is satisfiable.
Unsatisfiability is monotone under adding constraints:
\begin{equation}
  U\subseteq F'
  \quad\text{and}\quad V(U)=\varnothing
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad V(F')=\varnothing.
  \label{eq:core-monotonicity}
\end{equation}
Thus a learned core rules out every later candidate containing it.  Dually, if
$F'\subseteq F$ and $V(F)\neq\varnothing$, then $V(F')\neq\varnothing$.
Core extraction may use a Gr\"obner-basis certificate and subsequently delete
redundant constraints; the method section specifies the refinement procedure.

A \emph{structure hint} is an optional polynomial equation $h=0$.  Given a
finite hint set $H\subset R$, a branch selects some $A\subseteq H$ and studies
$P\cup A$.  Examples include sparsity constraints $x_i=0$ and affine relations
$a_1x_1+\cdots+a_nx_n=b$.  Since
\[
  A\subseteq A' \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  V(P\cup A')\subseteq V(P\cup A),
\]
an unsatisfiable hint core $U_H\subseteq H$, characterized by
$V(P\cup U_H)=\varnothing$, prunes every hint branch containing $U_H$.
Conversely, a satisfiable hinted branch defines a subvariety of $V(P)$ with the
requested structure.

Finally, for a satisfiable candidate $F$, its geometric dimension means the
Krull dimension of $R/\sqrt{\langle F\rangle}$, equivalently the dimension of
$V(F)$.  It can be read from the leading-monomial ideal of a Gr\"obner basis;
radical computation is not required because an ideal and its radical have the
same dimension~\cite{cox2015ideals}.
