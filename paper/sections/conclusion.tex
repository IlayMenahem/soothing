\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced a solver-agnostic framework that decomposes polynomial systems
into smaller branches by enumerating minimal hitting sets of their factor sets.
The framework can recursively split timed-out branches into the cases $x=0$ and $x\neq0$.

We proved that refinement terminates and preserves the full solution set.
Variable zeroing also preserves the solution set and terminates provided
that each timed solver call returns. The output is complete whenever no branches
remain unresolved.

On the baseline benchmarks, factor branching completed 19 of 20 systems, compared with
10 for direct Gr\"obner-basis methods, 17 for decomposition methods, and 14 for
the numerical method. On the QFT system, variable zeroing extends certified
completion from \texttt{facstd} alone to M4GB and Singular, improving
robustness to backend choice: three of six backends complete, versus one
with factor branching alone.

Future work will test this benefit on other systems, and improve the variable choice for splitting or find better splitting side conditions.
