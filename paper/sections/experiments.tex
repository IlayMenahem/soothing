\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{subsec:experimental-setup}

We implemented Algorithm~\ref{alg:Branching} in Rust 1.98.1 with Symbolica
3.0.1~\cite{ruijl2023symbolica}. Refinement prunes as in
Subsection~\ref{subsec:branching-framework}, and the branch solver computes a
Gr\"obner basis of each terminal branch, fewest generators first, with M4GB,
F5, GVW, F4, or Singular 4.4.1~\cite{makarim2017m4gb,faugere2002f5,
gao2016gvw,faugere1999f4,singular2025}; a basis containing $1$ certifies
UNSAT. The baselines are six whole-ideal Gr\"obner-basis configurations and
three decomposition methods from Singular and Macaulay2
1.26.06~\cite{singular2025,macaulay2}. Unless stated otherwise, every entry is one run on an Apple M4
Pro (14 cores, 24 GB) with a 60-second limit, and TO marks a timeout. Factor
branching times are wall times. The measurements are in the \texttt{results} directory of the
repository listed under Data availability.

\subsection{Baseline systems}
\label{subsec:baseline-systems}

The generated systems vary how many branches a system has and how hard they
are. The benchmark has 20 systems in six families
(Table~\ref{tab:benchmark-families}). Signed-distance cycles and affine-factor
hypergraphs have many cheap branches. Overlapping random multiples share dense
quadratic factors across equations. Gated systems multiply every equation of
Cyclic, Katsura, or a dense quadratic system by a new
variable~\cite{bjorck1991cyclic,boege1986examples}, so they have one easy
selector branch and one nonlinear residual branch.

\subsection{Size-graded families}
\label{subsec:scaling-families}

% [Families whose factors come from the problem, grown until every
% method times out]
The following families inherit their factors from the problem, and we
grow each until every method times out.
\begin{itemize}
  \item The reduced cyclic $n$-roots systems \texttt{Singular.rcyclic\_$n$}, a
    variant of the cyclic $n$-roots problem~\cite{bjorck1991cyclic}, for even
    $n$.
  \item Nash equilibria of $n$-player games with two pure strategies per
    player~\cite{sturmfels2002solving}.  Each player $i$ either plays a pure
    strategy, $p_i(p_i-1)=0$, or is indifferent, so its payoff difference
    vanishes.  The payoffs are random integers in $[-9,9]$ (generic) or in
    $\{-1,0,1\}$ (degenerate).
  \item Critical points of a random quadratic program $f$ on every face of the
    box $[0,1]^n$, given by $x_i(x_i-1)\,\partial_i f=0$.
  \item Equilibria of a random Lotka--Volterra community on every species
    support~\cite{hofbauer1998evolutionary}, given by
    $x_i\bigl(r_i+\sum_j a_{ij}x_j\bigr)=0$ with $a_{ii}<0$.
\end{itemize}
Each size is one seeded instance, and a method that times out is not run at
larger sizes. The direct class adds Singular's \texttt{std} in degrevlex
(Singular dp) and drops standalone F4.

Gr\"obner basis of the ideal, so it times out wherever \texttt{std} does, and
also on the generic Nash game with $n=4$, where \texttt{std} finishes.

\subsection{Physical system}
\label{subsec:physical-system}

The physical system is an order-five approximation that constrains graviton
couplings in asymptotic-safety models to remain consistent with quantum field
theory (QFT)~\cite{rafiezinedine2018simplifying,vacca2010asymptotic}; solving
it helps determine admissible fixed-point structures in quantum
gravity~\cite{vacca2010asymptotic}. It has 28 equations in 21 variables over
$\mathbb Q(\pi)$, and the coupling $\kappa_h$ must be nonzero.

Each run on the physical systems has a 120-second limit, or 300 seconds in
Table~\ref{tab:physical-decomposition}, runs concurrently with the others, and
reports its internal solver time. Factor branching and the Singular and
Macaulay2 baselines saturate by $\kappa_h$; the standalone M4GB, F5, GVW, and
F4 runs add the Rabinowitsch generator
$1-s\kappa_h$~\cite{rabinowitsch1930nullstellensatz}. Factor branching limits
its saturation to five seconds, removes $\kappa_h$ from every factor set, drops
a branch once $\kappa_h$ reduces to zero modulo its generators, and adds
$1-s\kappa_h$ to each terminal branch. Singular and Macaulay2 compute over
$\mathbb Q(\pi)$, except Macaulay2's \texttt{decompose}, which cannot; the Rust
programs take $\pi$ as a variable. Saturations use Singular's \texttt{sat} or
Macaulay2's \texttt{saturate}, primary decompositions use
\texttt{primdecSY}~\cite{shimoyama1996localization}, and
Algorithm~\ref{alg:PrimaryBranching} runs its separators. Refinement and
Algorithm~\ref{alg:IdealReduce} use lex order with the min-fill variable
order, a greedy elimination order computed from the variable supports of the
equations, and so do saturations and decompositions on the physical system.

All nine standalone baselines time out on the physical system, whereas factor
branching with each of its six backends, those of
Subsection~\ref{subsec:experimental-setup} and \texttt{facstd}, refines the
saturated system into 10 SAT terminal branches and solves them in 0.87 to 0.95
seconds, about 0.83 seconds of which go to the saturation.

% Nonzero conditions: the rounds keep the saturation instead of the ideal
Keeping $\kappa_h$ nonzero changes only the SAT test of
Algorithm~\ref{alg:IdealReduce}: a minimal hitting set $H$ counts as SAT when
$V(H)\not\subseteq V(\kappa_h)$. The proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:sat-branches}
then shows that $Q$ and the cofactors of $h$ have no common zero outside
$V(\kappa_h)$, so the Nullstellensatz gives
$\kappa_h^sh^{a_h}\in\langle Q\rangle$ for some $s\geq0$. Each round therefore
keeps $\langle Q\rangle:\kappa_h^\infty$ instead of $\langle Q\rangle$, and the
proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:ideal-reduce-termination} still applies.
Algorithm~\ref{alg:PrimaryBranching} then decomposes
$\langle Q\rangle:\kappa_h^\infty$, whose minimal primes are those of
$\langle Q\rangle$ that do not contain $\kappa_h$.

% [PrimaryBranching: the primary decomposition of the physical system]
Algorithm~\ref{alg:PrimaryBranching} decomposes the physical system in 271
seconds (Table~\ref{tab:physical-decomposition}). Ideal reduction turns the 28
equations into 18 generators in three rounds and 0.6 seconds, and prime factor
branching finds the six minimal primes in 0.1 seconds. The separators give the
isolated components in 1.6 seconds, but these do not intersect to the ideal,
so \texttt{primdecSY} decomposes the saturation of the reduced system, with 56
generators, in most of the remaining time. On the original equations with a
Rabinowitsch generator, \texttt{primdecSY} does not finish within 30 minutes.
The six isolated components form two families with dimensions 13, 12, and 8.
The components of one family are prime, and those of the other are obtained
from their radicals by squaring one generator, a variable, which doubles the
degree. Each family also has two embedded components, of dimension 10 inside
its 12-dimensional component and of dimension 8 inside its 13-dimensional
one. A Gr\"obner basis of the saturation confirms that the ten components
intersect to $\langle P\rangle:\kappa_h^\infty$.
