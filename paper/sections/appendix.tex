\section*{Appendix}

\subsection*{Simplifying expressions}

To simplify an expression, we can use the following local simplifications.
Some are always semantics-preserving, while others are valid only under
additional side conditions.

\paragraph{Always safe local simplifications.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $x_1 + \ldots + 0 + \ldots + x_n \mapsto x_1 + \ldots + x_n$
  \item $x_1 + \ldots + x + \ldots + (-x) + \ldots + x_n \mapsto x_1 + \ldots + x_n$
  \item $-(-x) \mapsto x$
  \item $x_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot 1 \cdot \ldots \cdot x_n \mapsto x_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot x_n$
  \item $x_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot 0 \cdot \ldots \cdot x_n \mapsto 0$
  \item $((-1)^{n_1} \cdot x_1) \cdot \ldots \cdot ((-1)^{n_k} \cdot x_k) \mapsto (-1)^{n_1 + \ldots + n_k} \cdot (x_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot x_k)$ where $n_i$ are integers
  \item $\frac{x}{1} \mapsto x$
  \item $\frac{x}{y} \cdot z \mapsto \frac{x \cdot z}{y}$
  \item $\frac{-x}{y} \mapsto -\frac{x}{y}$
  \item $\frac{x}{-y} \mapsto -\frac{x}{y}$
  \item $\frac{-x}{-y} \mapsto \frac{x}{y}$
  \item $\frac{x_1}{y_1} \cdot \ldots \cdot \frac{x_n}{y_n} \mapsto \frac{x_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot x_n}{y_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot y_n}$
  \item $\frac{\frac{x}{y}}{\frac{z}{w}} \mapsto \frac{x \cdot w}{y \cdot z}$
  \item $x^1 \mapsto x$
  \item $1^n \mapsto 1$
  \item constant folding for addition, multiplication, division, and powers whenever the result is defined, if the result is undefined give an error.
  \item flatten nested additions and multiplications: $x_1 + \ldots + (y_1 + \ldots + y_m) + \ldots + x_n \mapsto x_1 + \ldots + y_1 + \ldots + y_m + \ldots + x_n$
  \item combine terms: $y_1 \cdot x + \ldots + y_k \cdot x \mapsto (y_1 + \ldots + y_k) \cdot x$
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Local simplifications requiring side conditions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\frac{x}{x} \mapsto 1$ if $x \neq 0$
  \item $\frac{0}{y} \mapsto 0$ if $y \neq 0$
  \item $\frac{x \cdot y}{x \cdot z} \mapsto \frac{y}{z}$ if $x \neq 0$ and $z \neq 0$
  \item $\frac{x_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot x_i \cdot \ldots \cdot x_n}{y_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot x_i \cdot \ldots \cdot y_m} \mapsto \frac{x_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot x_{i-1} \cdot x_{i+1} \cdot \ldots \cdot x_n}{y_1 \cdot \ldots \cdot y_{i-1} \cdot y_{i+1} \cdot \ldots \cdot y_m}$ if $x_i \neq 0$ and $y_i \neq 0$
  \item $x^0 \mapsto 1$ if $x \neq 0$, or if $0^0$ is defined by convention
  \item $0^n \mapsto 0$ for positive integers $n$
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Local simplifications algorithm.}

To apply all the local simplifications, we use a fixed-point algorithm over the expression DAG. 
First we find all the local simplifications that can be applied to the DAG.
Then while there are still local simplifications that can be applied, we apply them and look at the changed nodes to see if they introduce new possible simplifications.

If there are multiple possible simplifications, we apply them in the following order of precedence:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Flattening:} For associative operations (addition and multiplication), flatten nested identical operations. This exposes all operands to the subsequent steps.
  \item \textbf{Combining like terms:} Group constants together (partial constant folding among siblings) and combine affine terms (e.g., $a\cdot x + b\cdot x \mapsto (a+b)\cdot x$).
  \item \textbf{Annihilators and Identities:} Apply rules involving $0$ and $1$ (e.g., $x \cdot 0 \mapsto 0$, $x + 0 \mapsto x$, $x^1 \mapsto x$).
  \item \textbf{Sign and Fraction Normalization:} Push negations down or pull them up consistently, and simplify fractions (e.g., $-(-x) \mapsto x$, $\frac{-x}{-y} \mapsto \frac{x}{y}$).
  \item \textbf{Constant folding:} If all operands are constants, immediately compute the result.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{DFS for linear elimination order}

In this setting, DFS means that we choose one available linear elimination, apply
it, and continue recursively on the reduced system until no further linear
elimination is possible. Only then do we backtrack and try a different
elimination choice from an earlier step.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
  \caption{DFS search for a linear elimination order}
  \label{alg:dfs-linear-elimination}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require Polynomial system $P$
    \Ensure A reduced system $Q$ together with a substitution map $\Sigma$
    \Function{DFSLinearElimination}{$P, \Sigma$}
    \State $L \gets \{(p, x) \mid p \in P,\ p \text{ is linear in } x\}$
    \If{$L = \emptyset$ or $P = \emptyset$}
    \State \Return $(P, \Sigma)$
    \EndIf
    \State $B \gets (P, \Sigma)$
    \ForAll{$(p, x) \in L$}
    \State solve $p = 0$ for $x$ to obtain $x \gets f(\hat{x})$
    \State $P' \gets P$ with $x \gets f(\hat{x})$ substituted in every equation
    \State remove zero equations from $P'$
    \State $\Sigma' \gets \Sigma \cup \{x \mapsto f(\hat{x})\}$
    \State $R \gets \Call{DFSLinearElimination}{P', \Sigma'}$
    \If{$R = (\emptyset, \Sigma'')$ for some $\Sigma''$}
    \State \Return $R$
    \EndIf
    \If{$R$ is better than $B$}
    \State $B \gets R$
    \EndIf
    \EndFor
    \State \Return $B$
    \EndFunction
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Here ``better'' means that the resulting system has fewer
remaining equations, and if the number of remaining equations is the same, then we prefer the system with more free variables.
