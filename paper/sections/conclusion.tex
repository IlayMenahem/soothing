\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced a solver-agnostic framework that decomposes polynomial systems
into smaller branches by enumerating minimal hitting sets of their factor sets.
The framework reuses certified UNSAT cores to prune branches and can recursively
split timed-out branches into the cases $x=0$ and $x\neq0$.

We proved that refinement terminates and preserves the full solution set.
Recursive splitting also preserves the solution set and terminates provided
that each timed solver call returns. The output is complete whenever no branches
remain unresolved.

On the baseline benchmarks, branching completed 19 of 20 systems, compared with
10 for direct Gr\"obner-basis methods, 17 for decomposition methods, and 14 for
the numerical method. On the QFT system, \texttt{facstd} was the only tested
backend to certify a complete result; the other backends recovered the
satisfiable branches but did not certify completeness.

Current experiments provide limited evidence about when recursive zero/nonzero
splitting improves completion. Future work will develop more effective splitting
strategies and more efficient ways to generate and reuse UNSAT cores.
